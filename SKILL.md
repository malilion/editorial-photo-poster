# Editorial Photo Poster

Create a separate poster for every uploaded photo. Never combine multiple source photos into one canvas or collage. If several photos are provided, process them independently and return one finished poster per photo.

When using an image-generation or image-editing tool, use the relevant uploaded photo as the reference for that poster. Make one generation/edit pass per source photo and keep other uploaded photos out of that pass. Do not replace the source with a text-only approximation when the photo is available.

## Composition

- Use a strict 3:4 vertical canvas.
- Split it horizontally into two exactly equal sections: the top half is 50% of the height and the bottom half is 50%.
- Make the two halves feel like one quiet, refined art-publication cover while preserving a clear contrast between photographic realism and handmade illustration.
- Do not allow a title, border, graphic, or illustration to move the dividing line away from the exact midpoint.

## Top half: photographic source

Preserve the original photograph as faithfully as possible. Keep the subject's identity, facial features, body proportions, pose, expression, clothing, objects, lighting, shadows, atmosphere, spatial relationships, and realistic texture unchanged. Apply only subtle premium editorial color grading. If the source needs to fit the format, extend only environmental areas such as sky, ground, walls, or surrounding background; extend them seamlessly and photographically. Never stretch, reshape, replace, or materially retouch the main subject.

## Bottom half: derived paper illustration

Reinterpret the most recognizable visual elements from the same photograph as a minimalist hand-drawn paper-cover illustration. Retain the recognizable subject, essential silhouette and proportions, key gesture or pose, important objects, and the core relationship between people and objects. Simplify aggressively: remove details that do not improve immediate recognition.

Use delicate, slightly imperfect drawn lines; a few bold acrylic-like flat color shapes; rough paper grain; visible handmade brush marks; and slightly irregular organic edges. Keep the main illustrated subject small, centered, and carefully composed, occupying approximately 10–20% of the bottom half, with generous negative space. Suggest the environment with only a few lines or restrained color shapes. The background should read as rough white, warm off-white, pale natural, or minimal editorial book-cover paper.

## Palette and typography

- Derive the illustration colors from the dominant colors in the source photo and compress them to no more than four main colors.
- Keep the palette restrained, sophisticated, harmonious, and flat rather than overly varied.
- Text is optional. Add only a small, understated editorial title, keyword, object name, location, year, number, or short phrase when it naturally belongs in the negative space. Never add filler copy or let typography compete with recognition and whitespace.

## Art direction and exclusions

Aim for quiet, poetic, refined, minimal, innocent, relaxed, artistic, thoughtful, high-recognition, and premium. The central idea is “a small subject surrounded by a large amount of empty space.” The result should feel like an independent art publication cover, not an advertisement.

Avoid colored-pencil, crayon, bleeding-watercolor, pure-line-art, complex realistic illustration, heavy oil paint, smooth polished digital illustration, 3D rendering, glossy 3D texture, commercial cartoon or cute character aesthetics, e-commerce styling, generic poster templates, decorative clutter, busy composition, and excessive typography.

## Final checks

Before presenting each result, verify visually that:

1. There is exactly one source photo represented in exactly one poster.
2. The canvas is vertical 3:4 and the horizontal split is exactly 50:50.
3. The top half remains recognizably photographic and faithful to the source.
4. The bottom half is a small, centered, paper-textured illustration derived from the same source, not a second realistic scene.
5. The illustration uses at most four dominant colors, preserves strong negative space, and contains no unnecessary text or decoration.
